# Ethical voting & outreach policy

This is the campaign's public, publishable policy for how votes and attention
are sought for ilies-bel.dev on Awwwards (and any similar gallery). It binds
everyone acting for the site — the owner, collaborators, and anyone asked to
share it. If a tactic isn't listed as permitted, ask; if it resembles anything
prohibited, don't.

## Prohibited — no exceptions

1. **No fake or duplicate accounts.** One person, one account, ever. No
   accounts created to vote, no household/office account farming.
2. **No requested scores.** We never ask anyone — publicly or privately — to
   give a specific score, a minimum score, or "full marks". Not jurors, not
   friends, not communities.
3. **No paid votes.** No purchasing votes, engagement, or review services of
   any kind, at any price, on any platform.
4. **No incentives.** No raffles, gifts, discounts, follow-backs, exposure
   promises, or favors in exchange for voting or rating.
5. **No vote swaps.** No "I'll rate yours if you rate mine" arrangements,
   explicit or winking, individually or in swap groups/rings.
6. **No bots or automation.** No scripted voting, no traffic bots, no
   engagement automation aimed at the listing.
7. **No juror lobbying.** No targeted contact with known jury members about
   the submission beyond what the platform itself provides for.
8. **No misrepresentation.** Credits name who actually did the work
   (co-authorship is already formalized on the site); no claiming solo work
   that wasn't, no hiding tooling or collaborators a juror would consider
   material.

## Permitted — and how

- **Announcing the submission** on the owner's own channels (site, GitHub, X,
  newsletter if any), linking the Awwwards page, with neutral wording:
  "It's submitted; if you like it, votes are open" — never score requests.
- **Permission-based outreach only.** Direct messages about the submission go
  only to people who have an existing relationship with the owner or have
  opted into hearing about the work. One message; no follow-up nagging; an
  easy implied "no" (silence closes the thread).
- **Answering questions honestly**, including about weaknesses. The site's
  own graveyard/limitations sections set the bar: if a juror asks what's
  weak, the answer is real.
- **Press/feature pitches** that describe the work factually (see
  `press-kit.md`), where the recipient chooses freely whether to cover it.

## Enforcement

- Anyone acting for the campaign who becomes aware of a violation reports it
  to the owner; the owner corrects it publicly if it affected votes (up to
  asking Awwwards to discount them).
- This file is versioned with the site. Changes to it are commits, visible in
  history — the policy can't quietly loosen.

*Rationale: the whole build argues that honest systems outlast loud ones. A
campaign that cheats would falsify the site's own thesis.*
