# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary audience is Awwwards jurors and creative-technology peers assessing concept, originality, execution, usability, and content as one body of work. They may arrive with limited time, compare the site against the best digital portfolios, and inspect both the visible experience and the engineering behind it.

Prospective collaborators and clients use the portfolio to judge Iliès Beldjilali's engineering taste, narrative judgment, and ability to finish ambitious interactive work. They need to move from the signature experience into concrete proof: shipped projects, technical writing, lessons from failed work, build notes, and a clear contact path.

The experience must also serve technically curious readers across desktop and mobile, including people on weak GPUs, people who prefer reduced motion, keyboard and assistive-technology users, and visitors whose browser cannot run WebGL.

## Product Purpose

This is Iliès Beldjilali's personal portfolio and technical publication. Its reverse stellar lifecycle turns a principle about durable software into an experience: the page begins with a black hole, rewinds through the life of a star, and resolves into a lone pale blue dot while the work, writing, failures, and story become available.

The site exists to demonstrate that cinematic narrative and exact engineering can reinforce each other. Success means the signature lifecycle is memorable at an award-calibre level, the full site feels authored as one world, and a visitor can still understand the work, read the content, evaluate the craft, and make contact without depending on spectacle.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, technical, exacting.

The voice is confident but not inflated, poetic only when the stellar metaphor earns it, and specific when discussing software or outcomes. The intended emotional arc is awe, curiosity, and pressure resolving into clarity and trust. The work should feel built by a person with strong judgment, not polished into an anonymous studio voice.

## Anti-references

- Generic developer-portfolio templates, repeated card walls, stock gradients, or decorative HUD styling that could belong to any technically themed site.
- Award-site spectacle that peaks on the home page while projects, writing, about, contact, and long-form reading feel like a separate conventional website.
- WebGL gimmicks that obscure content, hijack scrolling, conceal navigation, require precise pointer input, or fail when JavaScript, motion, or GPU capability is limited.
- Visual ambition without evidence: vague project claims, empty mockups, weak case-study detail, or interactions that do not reveal engineering judgment.
- Accessibility treated as a fallback tier rather than a complete expression of the same narrative and information.

## Design Principles

1. **Make the spectacle prove the craft.** The stellar lifecycle should demonstrate performance discipline, systems thinking, and narrative control, not merely decorate the portfolio.
2. **Keep the whole site in one world.** Home, projects, writing, articles, graveyard, build notes, about, and contact should share a coherent atmosphere and motion grammar while respecting the needs of each surface.
3. **Let the content survive the scene.** Every essential message and destination must remain clear through server-rendered text, semantic navigation, robust fallbacks, and calm reading surfaces.
4. **Show the full lifecycle.** Shipped work, unfinished ideas, failures, lessons, and implementation decisions belong together. Honesty is a stronger proof of judgment than a highlight reel.
5. **Earn wonder through precision.** Choreography, physics, typography, performance, accessibility, and interaction details must all withstand close inspection. Novelty counts only when it remains usable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across every user-facing route. Preserve semantic headings and landmarks, visible keyboard focus, complete keyboard navigation, readable contrast, meaningful link labels, and a reliable skip path around persistent chrome.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` without loading the live WebGL engine, and provide an understandable manual motion choice. Maintain complete no-JavaScript and no-WebGL paths with equivalent narrative copy and access to projects, writing, about, and contact. Do not make essential information, navigation, or reading progress depend on canvas visuals, hover, a custom cursor, sound, or color alone.
